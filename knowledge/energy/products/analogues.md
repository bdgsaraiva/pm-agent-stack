# Analogues: products

Past episodes that rhyme with a set-up. Each one is replayed day by day by agent 8 (`config/risk.yaml`; `python -m pmstack risk`), so figures come from the replay, never from memory.

| Episode | Dates | Why it rhymes | Replay id |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iraq invades Kuwait | Aug–Oct 1990 | supply shock with a precautionary premium | gulf_war_1990 |
| Desert Storm oil collapse | Jan 1991 | the premium gone in a day when the risk resolved | desert_storm_1991 |
| Commodity and inflation surge | Sep 2007–Jul 2008 | demand-driven run-up with flat supply (Hamilton 2009; Kilian 2009) | commodity_boom_2008 |
| Global financial crisis | May 2008–Feb 2009 | demand collapse | gfc_2008 |
| Oil collapse | H2 2014 | supply glut, OPEC lets prices fall | oil_collapse_2014 |
| Oil glut trough | Nov 2015–Jan 2016 | storage filling, deep contango | oil_trough_2016 |
| Covid crash and OPEC+ price war | Feb–Mar 2020 | demand shock plus supply war; WTI went negative in April | covid_2020, opec_war_2020 |
| Russia invades Ukraine | Feb–Jun 2022 | sanctions risk, product cracks spike | ukraine_2022 |
| Iran war, Hormuz closed | late Feb–Mar 2026 | chokepoint closure | war_2026 |
| Ceasefire, oil collapse | Apr 2026 | the premium unwinds on politics (Atlas: Andurand +31% Q1, −52% April) | ceasefire_2026 |

Lessons from the Atlas investor cards (Asset Management Atlas, People 3F): concentrated commodity bets can be wiped out by one political event (Andurand, 2026); being early is indistinguishable from being wrong if the fund can't survive the wait (Hall, 2014–17); "commodities refuse to trend forever", because supply responds to price (King). These are context for the critic's questions, not rules for the specialist.
