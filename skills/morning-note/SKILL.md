## Inputs (read in this order)
1. `notes/context.md`: standing facts he has stated; take them as given (e.g. dated Brent far above futures in 2026 is the war premium, not a data error).
2. `briefs/<today BRT>/facts.md`: the fact pack. **The only source of figures and links you may quote.** Sections you will use most: Alerts; **Market levels (table for the note)**; **News since the previous morning** (Top stories, Narrative check, stories by topic, each with outlet, time, narrative tags and the moves of the markets it touches); prices; US rates, credit and macro; Positioning (CFTC); Consensus map (stages, Since, Bursts, Quiet, Headlines); Narrative discovery; **Calendar (watching ladder)**; Balances; Data health.
3. `briefs/<today>/alerts.json`: ranked alerts with refs such as `alerts:2026-10-21#3`.
4. `briefs/<previous weekday>/note.md` if it exists: don't repeat it; say what changed.

If today's fact pack is missing, write three lines: date of the latest pack, which fetch failed (from `logs/runs.jsonl`), and that nothing is refreshed. Never fill gaps from memory or the web.

## Rules
- **Numbers:** every figure from the fact pack, with its link (or, for a news item, the item's own link) and its date when it isn't today. No other numbers. Moves as the pack gives them (% for prices, bp only where the pack gives bp).
- **News items:** only items listed in the fact pack. Write the fact first in a short plain sentence faithful to the headline (no claim, figure or name the headline doesn't carry), then the outlet linked: `… ([Reuters](url))`. Link the story's lead outlet; name at most one more ("reported by Reuters and Bloomberg"), or say how many carried it ("one of six outlets"), never the full list. Never invent a link or an outlet. Tier-4 outlets (aggregators: Benzinga, Motley Fool, Yahoo Finance republishing) are for finding stories only: never cite one unless a tier 1–3 outlet in the pack carries the same story, and then cite that one. Headlines from the Consensus map's GDELT lists are second choice: use them only when no outlet story covers the point, and name their site.
- **No opinion (Bernardo, 20 Oct evening).** The note holds only price data, news, narratives and the links between them. A link is either one the fact pack states (a story's narrative tag and its "moves"; the narrative check's price verdict) or plain co-movement said as such ("Brent -0.75% live as Gulf attack headlines rose"). No "Read:" lines, no chains of reasoning the news doesn't state (e.g. "the housing data feeds the Fed's hike debate"), no claims about what the market "treats as priced", no adjectives about the mood. "Because" only when a listed headline says so. The narrative verdict words below are the one judgement allowed, and they come from the news.
- **No trade instructions, sizes or targets.**
- **Data artefacts:** a front-month move far from its sister contract with no news (e.g. WTI vs Brent on a WTI expiry day, a roll) is said as "possible contract roll; not read as a move", not interpreted.
- Exceptions only in the digests: a market with nothing unusual gets no line. No emoji.

## Structure
Title: `Morning note — <Weekday> <D> <Mon> <YYYY>`

The **top block** (sections 1–3) stands alone: a reader who stops there has the day.

1. **Market levels** — the fact pack's "Market levels (table for the note)" copied as is: same rows, same order, same columns (`Market | Level | Change | sd`; group headers as bold rows). Code chose the rows (fixed ones, plus Henry Hub, wheat, silver, RBOB, ULSD, JGB, OAT, EUR/USD, USD/JPY and other optional rows only on a move of 1.5 sd or more or an alert); never add or drop a row. Levels are live unless the pack marks them `c` (closed today, e.g. Asia) or `y` (the previous session's close); keep those letters. Commodities are the most active contract, with its code. One source line under the table (the snapshot time from the pack's header). Nothing else: no list of rows left out, no notes on rolls, no commentary.
2. **What's happening –** the chain, in one paragraph or two:
   - Open with the biggest moves (from the table) in one plain sentence, no characterisation.
   - "In terms of incremental news, <n> developments since the previous note:" then each one in a sentence with its outlet link, from the pack's Top stories and topic lists (the story covered by the most outlets first; at most four; never a [price move] headline).
   - Tie each to price (the story's "moves", as co-movement) and to the narrative the pack tags it with. When price went the other way, say so as a fact ("Brent -0.75% live despite the attack headlines"). A large move that no story explains gets one clause: "no story in the pack covers it".
3. **Narratives: what's changing –** one block per narrative that has stories today, a burst or a stage change (most important first, at most six), from the pack's Narrative check and its per-narrative story lists:
   - **Judge direction from what the news says, not from how much there is.** Read each listed headline and decide which way it pushes the narrative, in the narrative's own words (the pack tags each story with them): Geopolitical risk **increase / decrease**; Oil and product supply **shortage / glut**; AI parts **bullish / bearish**; Central bank policy **hawkish / dovish**; US economy **strong / weak**; China stimulus **more / less**; Weak dollar **weaker / stronger**; Fiscal stress **worse / better**. The code's tag is a keyword pointer; overrule it when the headline says otherwise. Headline counts and the prior average are context only.
   - **How Bernardo reads headlines** (his direction check, 21 Oct; `docs/research/narrative-direction-audit/`): the main clause decides, not the background ("Elice jumps by limit after debut slump" = bullish; "Nscale boss seeks to reassure staff amid AI market fears" = bullish); a price-move headline counts only by its cause ("Oil rises amid attacks on vessels" = shortage; "Dollar index falls 0.25%" = nothing); refusing talks raises risk ("no talks with Washington until demands are met" = increase); visits, defensive deployments, statements welcoming mediation, polls confirming an expected path, funding rounds, valuations, lawsuits and bans = no direction; conflicts that don't touch his markets are off.
   - **Very** (the pack marks them "very bullish", "very increase", ...): highly relevant news that changes the picture, above all a surprise against expectations. Bernardo's test: "profit grew 30% as expected" is **not** very; "profit grew 5% against an expected 10% fall" **is**. A big number by itself never makes a story very. Examples from his check: a plan to restart strikes on Iran, $35 billion of Chinese Nvidia orders, a data-centre group falling 44% on a profit warning. Write "**very bearish:**" before such a headline and list it first under its direction; a very story weighs twice in the verdict. The pack's mark is a cue-word pointer: drop it when the headline isn't a surprise or a real change, and add it (at most one or two per narrative) when a headline plainly is one and the pack missed it.
   - **Central bank policy** covers the Fed, ECB and BoJ; the BoC, RBA and BOE only when the news is very relevant (a surprise decision or a clear break in guidance); never the PBOC (China stimulus), Bank of Israel or Bank Indonesia. Name the bank in each headline line ("hawkish (BoJ):"), and when banks point different ways, say so in the verdict ("mixed: BoJ hawkish, Fed dovish"). Its price check is per bank (the pack lists the markets of the banks in today's news): hawkish = that bank's short rate up and its currency stronger (Fed: 2y T-note future down, dollar index up; ECB: EUR/USD up, no euro short-rate series yet; BoJ: JGB 2y up, USD/JPY down; BoC: USD/CAD down; RBA: AUD/USD up; BOE: GBP/USD up). **Fiscal stress** covers the US, UK, France, Italy, Germany and Japan (no Brazil for now): name the country the same way; its price check uses US markets, so read another country's news as that country's only.
   - **Geopolitical risk** counts events with global implications that can move financial markets (the Middle East and Gulf shipping, Russia/Ukraine and NATO, China/East Asia, Korea, India/Pakistan, US military action, Venezuela, the shipping chokepoints); events in minor countries are off. Rising risk pushes gold, Brent, VIX and the dollar up. **Oil and product supply** counts disruptions and OPEC+ and SPR decisions: an OPEC+ cut or SPR refill = shortage, an OPEC+ hike or SPR release = glut. **US economy** strong = yields up and the dollar stronger.
   - One line: name — **verdict** — why, in a clause: stage and since (or "news feed only"), burst if any, and whether its markets moved its way (the pack's price verdict with the market and move, as a fact).
   - The verdict is one of the narrative's two words (e.g. **hawkish**, **glut**, **bearish**) when the news mostly points that way, or **mixed** when it points both ways; **new** for a discovery candidate flagged NEW that touches a focus market (label, sources, the market, its co-movement line verbatim from the pack, "Review: /narratives review").
   - Under the line, the news behind it: up to three headlines for each direction, each a short sentence with its outlet linked, marked with the direction word (e.g. "hawkish:", "dovish:").
   - **AI is one narrative with four parts** (Bernardo, 20–21 Oct; layers as in his AI monitor): **AI: applications** (software and model labs: IGV, SoftBank; OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI news), **AI: cloud compute** (neoclouds and hyperscalers: CoreWeave, Nebius, Applied Digital, Oracle, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta; Lambda, Crusoe, Nscale news), **AI: data centres** (data centres, power and chips: Vertiv, Digital Realty, NextDC, Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, Supermicro, TSMC, SK Hynix, Samsung, Kioxia, Tokyo Electron, Advantest), **AI: financing** (capital providers: BDCs, Blue Owl, Blackstone, Apollo, Ares, private credit). Write one **AI** block: a heading line, then one verdict line per part (bullish / bearish / mixed) with its news under it; a part with no stories today gets "no stories today". Each part's price check uses its own companies (the pack's "AI layers (companies)" table); you may name the one or two biggest company moves in a part, with figures from that table.
   - **AI hard data** (fact pack "AI demand signals", since 21 Oct 2026): under each AI part's verdict line add one **Data:** line with at most two figures from that part's rows (applications / cloud compute / data centres): value, date and a change exactly as printed (vs prev, 4w, 3m or 1y), with the row's source link. Prefer prints new since the previous note (Korea Customs 1-10/1-20 day and monthly chip exports, TSMC ~10th, Census ~1st, Ramp mid-month, GPU rentals weekly). Say in one clause whether the data points the same way as the news verdict; the data never overrides the news verdict by itself. Do not quote a row marked "(stale)" without saying so. DRAMeXchange figures are personal use: fine in this private note, never in a public post. If the section is missing or has no rows, skip the Data lines.
   - Live narratives: Geopolitical risk, Oil and product supply, AI (four parts), Central bank policy, US economy, Fiscal stress (these news feed only until their GDELT definitions are frozen), China stimulus, Weak dollar. Retired (don't use): Oil glut (now part of Oil and product supply), US fiscal stress (now Fiscal stress), Fed policy (now Central bank policy), Natural gas / LNG, Fed hikes and Fed cuts, Recession fears (now US economy), AI bubble, AI trade scare, Trade war / tariffs, US pump prices, Inflation scare, Russian oil sanctions, Hormuz / Iran oil supply, OPEC+, Copper shortage, Gold buying, Crop weather.
   - Say "narratives are unvalidated" once. No CFTC positioning line (Bernardo, 20 Oct evening). Never call a quiet narrative spreading or fading on the map's stage alone. A burst is never left out.
4. **What we'll be watching –** from the pack's "Calendar (watching ladder)": **Today**, **Next session**, **This week**, **Next week**, **Later**. Include watchlist earnings (with "before the US open" / "after the US close"), hand-entered events (conferences, keynotes), economic releases and central-bank decisions the calendar keeps (with ET and BRT times and the forecast when given), and Fed and other major central-bank speakers. **Countries: US, euro area, China and Japan only** (Bernardo, 20–21 Oct: no UK, Israel, Indonesia, Korea or other countries' data or central banks). No weekly reports (CFTC, EIA inventories, jobless claims). You may add a dated event named in a listed headline (same country rule), with its link. Never invent dates; an empty bucket is left out.
5. **Market in a Minute (macro) –** 4 to 8 items separated by semicolons, each a short fact with its outlet linked: Fed and rates, data, FX, fiscal, geopolitics and trade. Data and central-bank items from the US, euro area, China and Japan only (no UK, Israel, Indonesia, Korea or others). **Never a [price move] headline** (one whose point is only that a market rose or fell).
6. **Market in a Minute (commodities) –** 4 to 8 items: oil and products, gas and LNG, gold, silver, copper, aluminium, grains and softs (no nickel); company items only when they bear on a commodity (supply, refinery, mine, guidance). No [price move] headlines.
7. **Market in a Minute (equities and AI) –** up to 5 items, only where they bear on risk appetite, rates or commodity demand. No [price move] headlines.
7b. **Brazil fund industry –** only when the pack's "Brazil fund industry" watch has NEW items (since the previous morning): each in one short sentence with its outlet linked (managers arriving in or leaving Brazil, funds closing or returning capital, teams moving, new asset managers, stakes bought or sold), at most four. Older items are left out; no NEW items → no section. Read each headline: drop any that is not about an investment manager (the match is by keywords).
8. **Journal candidates.** Up to two alerts or stories that could become ideas, each with a ready line to paste, e.g. `/journal short RBOB? front -4.9% live — source alerts:2026-10-21#1`. The line only; no comment or question after it.
9. **Data health.** One line: failed or stale sources, possible contract rolls, news or calendar feeds that returned nothing, rows with no data (e.g. French OAT).

Length: the top block 350–550 words (the table not counted); the whole note at most 1,000 words.

## Delivery
1. Save the draft as `briefs/<today>/note.md` (title line first; no doc line yet).
2. **Number audit (agent 5).** `python -m pmstack audit briefs/<today>/note.md --step audit:note`. It checks every figure, date and alert ref against the fact pack and the journal, prints `problems`, and writes `note.audit.json`.
   - If there are problems, **fix each one once** from `facts.md`: change only that figure, date or ref (smallest edit; don't rewrite the note). A figure with no source in the pack is deleted, not guessed.
   - Re-run with `--flag`: anything still unmatched is listed in a banner at the top of the note ("Number audit (code): …"). Never remove the banner by hand.
3. Create a Docs page with the title above; the audited note is the body, banner included, with sources linked inline.
4. Add the first line `<!-- doc: <docs url> -->` to `note.md`.
5. Log: `python -m pmstack log --step note:morning --fields '{"asof":"<today>","version":"v7","outputs":["briefs/<today>/note.md"],"doc_url":"<url>","alerts_used":["<refs>"],"news_items":<n linked>,"outlets":["<outlets cited>"],"audit":{"checked":<n>,"orphans":<n>,"fixed":<n>}}'`
6. `git add briefs logs && git commit -m "note: <today>" && git pull --rebase && git push`
7. Final message (the phone notification), three short lines: (1) the dominant theme in a few words, (2) and (3) the two biggest narrative calls (e.g. "Central bank policy: hawkish (RBA)") or moves, then the Docs link. If the banner is there, start line 1 with "Audit: <n> unmatched figures".
